Bring your own models

You choose the model for each job.

JimmyHQ does not come with a model or an account of its own. You add the ones you want, and give each of the {{role_count}} roles a first choice and fallbacks. Every part of JimmyHQ asks for a role, and no model name is written into the code.

On your machine

Any server that speaks the OpenAI-compatible API, such as {{providers:local}}. Or a second computer of yours, with a graphics card, that lends its models.

Hosted, with your own key

{{providers:hosted}}. Your own model is used first, and the screen says when a hosted one answers.

{{roles}}

What makes it different

Four ideas the code holds to.

Local first

Your data is one folder on your machine.

JimmyHQ listens on your own computer only, unless you open it to a private network. Everything it keeps is in one data folder, outside the code. Once you choose where, it backs that folder up on a schedule and tests each backup.

  • tape.dbthe record of every decision
  • memory/notes you can read and edit
  • vault/large content, by its hash
  • apps/each app's files and data
  • secrets.jsonyour keys, owner-only

One owner

It has one owner, and that is you.

No team accounts and no account with us. You sign in with a password stored as a hash. Tokens you make for other tools are readers or writers, never the owner, and you can revoke any of them.

The Bench

Agents bring decisions to you.

Sending, posting, paying and deleting wait for your click unless you have allowed that one action. Only your own click records a decision: a token, an agent or an app never decides.

Apps in a sandbox

An app does only what you agreed to.

A sandboxed app is a page in a locked frame, with no network of its own. You read what it may do before you install, in these words. A full app runs as you, and the dialog sets that apart as a warning.

Get it

Five commands, then your browser.

You need uv and Node.js 22 or later. Then open the address it prints, 127.0.0.1:8440, and create your owner account.
